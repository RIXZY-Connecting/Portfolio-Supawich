# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Clients and recruiters looking to hire a freelance front-end developer. They visit from a recruiter inbox, a LinkedIn referral, or a portfolio link in a job application. The context is professional evaluation: they want to understand what Supawich can build, how he thinks, and whether his aesthetic sense matches what they need. The job to be done is forming a confident hiring decision in a single visit.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Supawich "RYU" Sriviboonruttana, an ECT graduate and front-end developer based in Thailand. The site exists to convert qualified leads into contact — whether that's a freelance contract, an internship, or a full-time role. Success means a visitor says "I want to work with this person" before they reach the contact section.

## Brand Personality

Precise, engineered, alive. The brand feels like a developer who takes craft seriously but hasn't lost curiosity. Not corporate — there's a person here. Not chaotic — every choice is intentional. The emotional goal: quiet confidence with a flicker of technical delight.

## Anti-references

- Bootstrap template portfolios: generic, cookie-cutter, forgettable
- Minimal white with a single accent color: too safe, no statement, invisible in the market
- Heavy glassmorphism as the primary surface language: overused in 2024-2025, reads as AI-generated

## Design Principles

1. **Show craft through execution, not claims.** Every component, transition, and detail should demonstrate front-end ability without a word of copy saying so.
2. **One idea per fold.** Generous section spacing, deliberate pacing. Let each section breathe before the next arrives.
3. **The terminal is a cultural object, not a costume.** Monospace type and code aesthetics are part of Supawich's real practice; use them with precision, not decoration.
4. **Committed palette over safe palette.** The teal carries the brand. Don't hedge it with too many neutrals; use it with confidence.
5. **Product-grade interaction.** Hover states, transitions, and focus styles should feel like a polished product UI, not a static brochure that someone bolted animations onto.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum (4.5:1 body text contrast). Reduced motion respected via @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce). No color-only information.
